The whole product as connected screens: an interleaved Channel A & B journey split across three resumable sessions, a facilitator review gate before any report is generated, the points where students upload their portfolio and resume, and the admin console for questions, tasks, images, tags, and rubrics.

Change 1 · Interleave
Stated (A) and behavioural (B) modules alternate instead of A-then-B. For the most game-prone traits — values, aspiration — the behavioural task runs first (B1 budget before A3 values; B5 wish-sort before A7 aspiration), so the stated answer can't retrofit itself to the behaviour. Watch the blue/orange chips alternate through the flow.
Change 2 · Split, sealed sessions
Capture is broken into three resumable sessions of ~15–18 minutes. Each session seals on completion: once submitted, its answers — especially behavioural ones — are locked and can't be reopened or edited. A returning student resumes at the next item and never sees prior behavioural responses, so one session can't contaminate another.
Also captured · Direction vs. Market
The wish-sort runs a third pass — "what pays best" — sealed last so money can't prime the wish and actual passes, and A7 asks what the student believes the market rewards. Together these surface the passion-vs-pay tension (e.g. product design vs better-paid UI/UX) that the report names in its new Direction vs. Market view.

Change 3 · Facilitator review gate
No report is generated automatically. A facilitator checks each submission — especially the surprises and the coherence adjudication, the highest-stakes claims — then approves, lightly edits, or flags before the engine generates and releases. During the pilot this keeps a human between raw data and a student's identity report.
