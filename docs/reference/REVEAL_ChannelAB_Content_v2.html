Part One

Channel A · stated modules

Capacity tags: EM Empathic · AN Analytical · AE Aesthetic · SY Systems-sensing · NA Narrative · CV Conviction. AN = decompose / logic / evidence; SY = wholes / flows / ripple effects. ★ = non-design domain (orthodoxy guard).
A1

Capacities

9 cover-and-normalise items

Each item carries only the capacities that fit it naturally (4–6 options) — forcing all six everywhere would manufacture nonsense choices (e.g. "chase the beauty" of a community problem). Instead every capacity appears as a real option in ≥3 items.

PromptOptions → capacity
1 · You're handed a project you genuinely care about. Before anything else, you find yourself…talking to the people it's for EM · working out the underlying logic AN · picturing how it should look & feel AE · seeing how the whole thing connects SY · getting the story it must tell right NA · clarifying what it should stand for CV
2 · ★ A neighbour's well-loved old chair has broken; they ask your help. You first…ask what the chair means to them EM · work out exactly how it failed AN · notice its form & proportion AE · get curious where it came from NA · decide if it's worth saving CV
3 · ★ A town asks you to improve its weekly market. Your instinct is to…talk to stallholders & shoppers EM · map the flow, find bottlenecks AN · see the market as one connected system SY · find its character & play it up NA · decide what it should mean to the town CV
4 · You walk into a museum exhibit that clearly isn't working. You first notice…visitors look lost, unhelped EM · the sequence makes no sense AN · it's visually clumsy — poor proportion, no beauty AE · the parts don't add up to a whole journey SY · it tells no story NA · it believes in nothing CV
5 · ★ A friend asks you to help plan a once-in-a-lifetime event. You're most drawn to…what each guest needs to feel EM · the schedule & logistics AN · the look & atmosphere of the space AE · how every part flows into the next SY · the moment it builds toward NA · what the day is really about CV
6 · You're given an unfamiliar object: "make this better." You start by…imagining who it fails EM · breaking down each part's job AN · seeing how it fits the wider system it lives in SY · rethinking what it's for NA · questioning if it should exist CV
7 · ★ A community has a problem nobody's solved. You'd begin by…living alongside them EM · studying why past tries failed AN · seeing the whole system around it SY · finding the story the data misses NA · being honest what you can change CV
8 · A free weekend to make something just for you. You'd likely…make something for someone you love EM · solve a nagging puzzle AN · make something beautiful for its own sake AE · write/film/tell something NA · make something that says what you believe CV
9 · You're composing a space for a photo. Your first instinct is…read the mood people want EM · work out the lighting logic AN · chase proportion, colour, beauty AE · arrange how every element relates SY · build the story the image tells NA · decide what feeling it must stand for CV
Scoring · A-score(capacity) = times chosen ÷ times it appeared (not ÷ item count) — items carry different option sets. Coverage: EM 9 · AN 9 · CV 9 · NA 8 · SY 6 · AE 6 appearances. Feeds the spike chart.
Tuning · 9 richer items may run long; trim to 7 once the pilot shows which items best carry the new AE / SY capacities. Craft is no longer here — it moved to capabilities (A7).

Perceived market (new — locates the passion-vs-pay tension, e.g. product design vs UI/UX):

  • "Which kinds of design do you think pay best — and which pay least?" (rank: product design · UI/UX · visual/graphic · branding · service design · UX research · industrial · motion · social-impact · strategy) → the market model the student is operating on
  • "The work that most interests me and the work that pays best are…" → the same · a little apart · pulling in opposite directions (single self-location)
Treat as belief, not fact · early students' market knowledge is often shaky or inherited. The report examines this perception, it doesn't take it as true — and should eventually be backed by real market data.
Scoring · desired vector = stated side of the gap. Perceived-market rank + stance feed the Direction-vs-Market view. Current capability comes from the portfolio, not here — see the schema fix.

B3

First-three-moves

specced

Brief (unfamiliar domain) · "A small coastal town finds fewer young people stay after school age. They've asked for help. You have no further information. What are your first three moves?"

Palette (8, drag 3 in order) · talk to the people affected · study how other towns handled it · sketch/make something quickly · tighten what the real problem is · build a small thing to test · look at the data · question whether the brief is right · find everyone with a stake. Then one line: "the first question you'd ask."

Archetype map · research-led (talk/study/data) · maker-led (sketch/build) · reframer (tighten/question) · systems-led (stakeholders)

Tags · Capacities ● · Roles ●
Scoring · position-weighted (1st=3, 2nd=2, 3rd=1) → archetype; first move dominant. Keep a 2nd brief (children's hospital waiting room) to rotate against memory.
B4

Attention capture

specced · needs 5 images

Mechanic · 5 information-rich scenes, one at a time, ~8s each. "Tap the 3 things that jump out." Records which tagged category-zones are hit, and in what order.

The 5 categories (= A5 taxonomy): PEOPLE/emotion · FORM/aesthetics · SYSTEM/flow · DETAIL (a small telling thing) · TEXT/story.

SceneMust visibly contain — one element per category
1 · Street marketvendors/shoppers mid-interaction (PEOPLE) · colour/light/composition (FORM) · stall & queue flow (SYSTEM) · a hand-painted sign or odd object (DETAIL) · shop names, price boards (TEXT). Avoid empty/staged.
2 · Workshop benchhands working or clear human trace (PEOPLE) · material textures (FORM) · tools laid in sequence, WIP stages (SYSTEM) · a worn tool, a taped note (DETAIL) · labels, handwriting (TEXT). Avoid pristine flat-lays.
3 · Transit concoursetravellers rushing/waiting/lost (PEOPLE) · architectural scale & light (FORM) · wayfinding & people-flow (SYSTEM) · dropped ticket, someone's luggage (DETAIL) · signage, departure boards (TEXT). Avoid empty.
4 · Community hall, mid-eventgroups engaged, social dynamic (PEOPLE) · room character (FORM) · seating/stations/queue organisation (SYSTEM) · a child's drawing, a tea table (DETAIL) · banners, name tags, agenda (TEXT). Avoid formal empty.
5 · Product still-lifeowner's traces, no face needed (PEOPLE) · deliberate composition & materiality (FORM) · grouping logic (SYSTEM) · one characterful out-of-place object (DETAIL) · packaging, labels, a note (TEXT). Avoid minimalist styled shots.

Zone-tagging scheme · overlay each image with invisible category polygons; a tap records the category-zone hit + order.

scene_asset = {
  stimulus_id: "s1_market",
  zones: [
    { category:"PEOPLE", polygon:[[x,y]…] },
    { category:"FORM",   polygon:[[x,y]…] },
    { category:"SYSTEM", polygon:[[x,y]…] },
    { category:"DETAIL", polygon:[[x,y]…] },
    { category:"TEXT",   polygon:[[x,y]…] }
  ]   // 2–3 zones per category · non-overlapping · area-balanced
}
Tags · Capacities ● · Salience ● · Storytelling ○
Validity · zones must be area-balanced across categories — if PEOPLE zones cover twice the screen of TEXT, you manufacture a "people-noticer" that isn't real. Balance area, not just presence. A tap outside all zones = uncategorised (kept as signal).

B6

Bring three things (upload)

specced · student uploads

Prompt · "Bring 3–5 images of things you can't stop looking at. Not your own work — anything: a building, an object, a photo, a scene, a piece of design, something from nature. For each, one line: why it pulls you." ("can't stop looking at" not "that inspire you" — inspiration invites performance.)

Coding rubric · each image scored on hidden axes (vision model, or hand-tagged for the pilot):

DimensionPolesSurfaces
subjecthuman-present ↔ object/emptywhether people are the draw
erahistorical/aged ↔ contemporaryheritage vs. now
complexityornate/dense ↔ minimal/sparevisual appetite
emotionwarm/intimate ↔ cool/formalaffective register
originnatural ↔ built/madewhere beauty is found
narrativestory-laden ↔ purely formalis there an implied story
crafthandmade/imperfect ↔ precise/manufacturedvalue on the human hand

Plus a free theme tag from the A5/A6 taxonomy, so it cross-references the instrument. Output = a consistent lean across the set, shown back as a question, never a verdict.

b6_result = {
  images: [ { ref, why, tags:{subject:+1, era:−0.6, narrative:+1 …}, theme:[…] } ],
  detected_thread: ["human-present","story-laden","historical"],
  thread_statement: "These seem to share a human story from another time —",
  confirm_question: "does that feel true?", confirmed: null
}
Tags · Salience ● · Obsessions ● · Storytelling ○ · Craft ○
Scoring · the cross-image pattern is the signal — one image is curatable, a lean across 4–5 is hard to fake. Confidence scales with how many images share the thread (5/5 strong, 3/5 tentative).
Pilot shortcut · a facilitator hand-tags uploads against the rubric before the thread is shown — fully validates the task & rubric without building the vision pipeline. Automate only once the rubric reliably produces threads students confirm.

B8

Disruption task

specced · the 8th

Mechanic · a 3-minute micro-task — "rough out an approach to [small brief]" in a text/sketch box. At ~60% of the timer, the rug moves via modal: your main approach is now disallowed, or a hard constraint is added ("must work with no screen"). Observe the response, not the output. A second, milder disruption de-noises. One-tap "how did that feel?" after.

Tags · Conditions ● · Conviction ○ · Aspiration ○
Captured · {disruptions:[{response:enum[abandon,adapt,reframe], recovery_ms, generated_new:bool}], felt}. Scoring · abandon/freeze vs adapt vs reframe + recovery + new-options → fixed/growth + imperfection tolerance.
Cautions · one disruption is noisy (hence two) · a low-stakes lab failure isn't a real one, so it's a weak-but-real signal · keep it light — must not tip into distress.
