Before the steps · what kind of gap?skill vs. way-of-thinking

Are your gaps skills you can build, or wiring you'd have to grow?

Where you want to go — social-impact, human-centred design — needs two kinds of things: certain ways of thinking (capacities) and certain skills (capabilities). Before we suggest anything, here's the key question: do you already have the ways of thinking it needs?

Capacities your direction needs — and you have
  • Empathy — your thickest network (92). Your whole direction rests on it.
  • Conviction (72) — you know what your work should stand for.
  • Systems-sensing (70) — enough for service & community work.
Gaps — all capabilities (learnable)
  • Field research · 20→90 · Real
  • Venture-building · 40→85 · Real
  • Facilitation · 30→80 · Wish
  • Systems & service design · 50→70 · Wish
Good news: nothing about how you think is in your way — your mind is already suited to where you want to go. Every gap you have is a skill, and skills can be learned. The next part is how.
If a gap had been a way of thinking instead of a skill, a course wouldn't fix it. You'd have three honest choices: grow it slowly over months with practice and a mentor; team up with someone who has it; or rethink the direction if you don't want to spend years building it. Good to know — but that's not your situation.
The science. Skills (capabilities) are trainable on a timescale of weeks-to-months; cognitive leanings (capacities) shift slowly through sustained practice. Naming which kind a gap is determines whether a course can close it.◐ Well-motivated · plasticity timescales differ by construct
